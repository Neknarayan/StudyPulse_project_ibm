1. Problem Statement

In many colleges, a student who is falling behind is noticed only after poor exam results, when there is little time left to intervene. Teachers handle large classes and cannot manually track every student's attendance, assignments, and study habits. This delays timely support and contributes to preventable course failures and dropouts, directly impeding progress toward SDG 4.

2. Proposed Solution

StudyPulse is an AI-assisted early-warning web application. It ingests routine student indicators, applies a trained machine learning model to estimate each student's probability of failing, groups them into High (≥60%), Medium (35–60%), and Low (<35%) risk, explains which factors drive the risk, and suggests practical support actions. An interactive what-if tool allows teachers to simulate risk adjustments.

3. Project Objectives

Early Risk Detection:

Predict at-risk students before examinations using data readily available during the semester.

Teacher Prioritisation:

Rank students by urgency so educators allocate limited counselling time where impact is highest.

Transparent Explainability:

Explain every prediction in plain language instead of displaying a black-box numerical score.

Actionable Support:

Map primary risk factors to practical, human-reviewed pedagogical interventions.

5. Methodology & Pipeline

  1. Data Generation & Ingestion: Synthesize or load CSV; validate schemas.
  2. Exploratory Data Analysis: Examine correlations between attendance, grades, and risk.
  3. Train/Test Partitioning: Stratified 80/20 train/test split (480 train / 120 test).
  4. Feature Standardisation: Apply StandardScaler to eliminate scale biases.
  5. Model Training: Train balanced Logistic Regression and Random Forest benchmark.
  6. Evaluation: Calculate Accuracy, Precision, Recall, F1, ROC-AUC, & 5-fold CV.
  7. Local Explanation: Explain predictions via linear feature contributions ($coef \times z$).
  8. Action Recommendations: Link top contributors to pedagogical interventions.

Rationale: Logistic Regression was selected for active deployment over Random Forest because its Recall is significantly higher (0.744 vs 0.651), which minimizes missed at-risk students, and its mathematical structure enables transparent per-student feature attributions.

8. Innovation

Rather than delivering raw statistical scores, StudyPulse transforms predictions into an actionable intervention cockpit:

  • Prioritised cohort triage list focusing educator attention on highest urgency.
  • Instant plain-language explanations identifying the root drivers.
  • Evidence-backed pedagogical intervention suggestions per risk factor.
  • Interactive what-if scenario simulator for goal setting and mentoring.

9. Ethics & Responsible AI

  • Human-in-the-Loop: Predictions serve as decision support; teachers make all final decisions.
  • Privacy: Uses anonymised IDs (S001, etc.); no personally identifiable information (PII) required.
  • Fairness & Bias: Avoids protected demographic attributes (gender, race, ethnicity).
  • Supportive Stance: Flags trigger supportive academic interventions, never punitive measures.

10. Current Limitations

  • Trained on synthetic prototype data; requires real-world institutional validation.
  • Represents a single-point snapshot rather than continuous time-series trajectories.
  • Intervention recommendations are currently rule-based heuristics.

11. Future Scope

  • Multi-semester real anonymized dataset validation.
  • Weekly trend analysis on attendance dips and quiz scores.
  • Feedback loop capturing which interventions succeeded.
  • Direct integration with Canvas, Moodle, and Blackboard LMS.
  • Automated early alerts via SMS / email to advisors.

12. Conclusion

StudyPulse demonstrates that standard academic indicators can be harnessed into an early, explainable warning system that helps educators intervene early. Directly advancing SDG 4, it bridges the gap between AI predictive modeling and practical educational support.
